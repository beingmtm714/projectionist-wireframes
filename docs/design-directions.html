Settled. Direction A, the Projection Booth, was chosen, and DESIGN.md in the projectionist-wireframes repo was rewritten from the redrawn artboards. This is the record of the argument, not a live question. Read DESIGN.md for what the design language actually is.

Written 28 August 2026, before any rewrite of DESIGN.md. Michael's diagnosis is right: French New Wave typography, blockbuster curtains and an Old Hollywood leader are all cinema, and together they are a props cupboard rather than a system.

The reframe that decides most of it

The system's job is to be the room, not the film.

A great repertory house in Los Angeles has enormous personality and does not change it when the programme changes. The Vista is the Vista whether it is showing Murnau or a midnight Hangover 2. The room is the constant, the film is the variable, and the audience understands that arrangement without being told.

Every worry about hosting Caligari and The Notebook in one system dissolves at that sentence. The chrome is never commenting on the film. It is the place you came to watch it.

There is a mechanical version of this worth adopting whichever direction wins: the film's own identity appears in exactly one place, the one-sheet, and nowhere else. The marquee changes weekly. The building does not.

The second reframe: Expressionism is a lighting model, not a theme

Michael's instinct toward German Expressionism and noir is stronger than it may look, and it is strongest if we take the technique rather than the costume.

The lasting contribution of Caligari is not crooked sets. It is that light and shadow do the emotional work, and shadow is an active agent rather than an absence. Noir is that same technique pointed at moral ambiguity: a hard key, deep falloff, things half seen. Sunset Boulevard is lit the way it is because the film is about what a house conceals.

That maps onto this product almost too neatly. A curator takes an audience past the surface of a film into what is actually going on underneath it. The design can enact that rather than illustrate it.

And the existing DESIGN.md is already halfway there without saying so. "Light, not paint" is a lighting model. Electric silver rationed to the live signal, with a two-layer glow, is a key light. What sits on top of it is memorabilia.

Direction A — The Projection Booth

Thesis. The app is a dark room with one source of light. Everything legible is legible because something is illuminating it. Shadow is structural: surfaces have direction, elements emerge from darkness rather than sitting on a flat ground.

Keeps. The night ground. Electric silver as the only live signal, and its glow. Hairlines. Source Serif as the curator's voice. Timecode mono.

Kills. Bulb gold as marquee nostalgia. The scalloped and interstitial curtains. The Academy leader. The Cinema Paradiso lobby.

Replaces them with. Two lighting states rather than three temperatures: house up for everything before and after, a warm dim ambient with no source visible, and house down for the show, near black with a single cold key. T-0 is not a curtain parting; it is the house going dark and the beam striking. Raking light and cast shadow become the layout device, so a Liner Note arrives lit rather than boxed.

The Hangover 2 test. Passes most cleanly of the three. A dark room and a beam are the apparatus of cinema, not a genre of it. Nobody reads a projection booth as editorialising.

Populism. Also the strongest. Everyone has been in a dark room when the lights go down. No one needs to have heard of Murnau to feel it. The reference is invisible to the people who would not get it and legible to the people who would.

The honest tradeoff. Dark interfaces with glow are everywhere, and this fails if the shadow work is not genuinely disciplined. The difference between this and every other dark mode is whether light appears to have a direction and a source. That is real craft, and if it is executed at eighty percent it reads as generic.

Direction B — The Rep House

Thesis. The building is the brand. Deco-adjacent architecture, oxblood and brass and terrazzo, the physical grandeur of a 1930s picture palace that now shows whatever it likes. The grandiosity is the joke and the sincerity at once, which is exactly how those houses actually programme.

Keeps. Gold, curtains and the marquee, but reorganised as architecture rather than scattered as nostalgia. The lobby and the marquee become genuinely load-bearing rather than decorative.

Kills. French New Wave typography. The leader. The cold electric signal, which does not belong in a warm room.

The Hangover 2 test. Passes, by the same logic a real rep house does. The room's grandeur is a constant that the programming plays against, sometimes in tribute and sometimes against the grain.

Populism. Warm and immediately readable. A picture palace is not an intimidating reference.

The honest tradeoff. It is the most expensive to execute and the least forgiving. Deco done at eighty percent is not neutral, it is a wedding venue. It also fights the product's actual behaviour: most of the time the user is on a phone in a dark living room, and a warm gilded interface at that moment is the wrong amount of light in the room. And it is the direction furthest from Michael's stated instinct.

Direction C — The Contact Sheet

Thesis. This is a criticism product, so build it from the apparatus of film scholarship. Frame stills, contact sheets, marginalia, timecode, the register of Sight and Sound and Criterion essays. Type-led and image-led, restrained, almost no chrome.

Keeps. The serif as the curator's voice, the mono timecode, hairlines, the discipline of section 8.

Kills. Nearly everything theatrical. Curtains, bulbs, the leader, the ON AIR sign.

The Hangover 2 test. Passes by neutrality. A contact sheet does not care what is on it.

Populism. The weakest, and the most self aware about it. It says "you are studying this film," which is the correct register for exactly the audience least in need of persuading and the wrong one for everybody else.

The honest tradeoff. It throws away the event. The product's whole emotional claim is that this is a night, in a room, happening now, with other people. Editorial neutrality turns it into a reading app with a clock. It would be the right answer for a different product, and it is worth having on the table because it is the direction that most reduces execution risk.

What I would pick, and why

A, the Projection Booth, and not narrowly.

It is the only one of the three that wins on both axes rather than trading them against each other. It is the most distinctive to a cinephile and the most legible to someone who has never heard of Caligari, because a dark room going darker is universal and the Expressionist technique underneath it is invisible unless you know to look. B is warmer but fights both the phone and the founder's instinct. C is safest and gives up the thing the product is selling.

It is also the smallest distance from what already exists. The tokens barely move: the night ground stays, the silver stays, the glow stays. What changes is that the memorabilia comes out and a lighting model goes in behind what remains, which means the ten artboards need reworking rather than redrawing.

Two things I would keep arguing about even after picking A.

The Academy leader is the most beloved and most populist artefact in the current set. Everyone recognises a countdown on film. Cutting it as memorabilia may cost more than it gains, and there is a version where the fifteen seconds before T-0 is a light coming up to intensity rather than numbers, which keeps the ritual and drops the pastiche.

And oxblood. Under A it has no obvious job, since the curtain goes. It may be worth keeping as the single warm accent purely so the palette is not entirely cold, in which case it needs a new and specific role rather than a leftover one.

What happens next, if A is chosen

Not a rewrite of DESIGN.md in place. The order that works:

  1. Agree the lighting model in words, including what house up and house down actually mean in tokens.
  2. Redraw two artboards against it, one resting and one live, as a test rather than a commitment. The Room and the Marquee are the right pair.
  3. Only then rewrite DESIGN.md, from the redrawn pair rather than from the argument.
  4. Then the remaining eight artboards, then _kit.txt, then src/tokens.css.

Doing it in that order means the design system is written from something that exists, which is how the current one got good, rather than from a description of something intended.
